A daily devotional practice

Five quiet minutes, kept every morning

A short guided practice for people who already pray, and who have felt that the habit slipped away somewhere between the alarm and the first message of the day.

Why five minutes

Most people who want a steadier prayer life do not stop because they stopped believing. They stop because the practice they were handed asked for more time than an ordinary week allows — an hour before dawn, a chapter a day, a discipline built for a quieter century than this one.

This practice asks for a few minutes, anchored to something you already do without fail: the first cup of the morning. You sit, you listen, you name what you are asking for, and you write a single line before you stand up. Nothing is added to your schedule. Something already in it is simply given a different weight.

Step one

Settle the room

Headphones on, phone on airplane mode, hands open on your knees. Thirty seconds.

Step two

Open with words

A short prayer, spoken aloud, and one clear request named in a single sentence.

Step three

Listen

A few minutes with your eyes closed. No forcing, no concentrating. If your mind wanders, come back.

Step four

Seal and note

Thirty seconds of silence when the audio ends, then one written line in a notebook.

On keeping a record

The written line matters more than it looks. Memory holds on to what hurts and lets almost everything consoling slip away — which is why people who have received a great deal can still feel they have received nothing. A notebook is what turns a week of small things into something you can actually read back.

Questions people ask

Do I have to belong to a particular church?

No. The practice is Christian in language and in the prayers it uses, and it is kept by people from many traditions. Nothing in it requires membership anywhere.

What if I feel nothing?

That is common in the first days, and for some people it stays that way while the practice still does its work. Feeling is a measure of sensitivity, not of effect. The notebook is the honest measure, read at the end of a week.

How long before it becomes a habit?

The practice is built in seven-day cycles because that is long enough to see a pattern and short enough to finish. Most people decide somewhere in the second week.

Is this a treatment for anything?

No. It is a devotional practice and nothing more. It is not medical, psychological or financial care, it does not diagnose or treat any condition, and it should never take the place of professional help or prescribed treatment.
